# kebab — the visual world
### Art direction doctrine · v1 — companion to §13 of the foundation
*Built from the maker's references: the painted canopy with white birds; the grass fields under a data-annotated sky (empty, and with a lone observer); the blue-on-cream halftone villas; the Klein-blue airbrushed hands.*

---

## 0. The thesis, in the maker's words

**A more beautiful future through thoughtfulness.**

Tech-forward but human and intentional. An advanced future that is neither cyberpunk nor dystopian — and, just as deliberately, not nostalgic. The future looks like a meadow you can still stand in. The technology is present, but it has manners.

Working name for the system: **the noticed world.**

---

## 1. What the references actually share

Read as a set, five properties repeat. These are the rules of the world:

1. **Every image is a picture of attention.** A loggia framing the sea. A figure facing the horizon. Birds observed from above. The subject is never a device and never a user — the subject is *looking itself*. This is "people paying attention to their lives" as art direction.

2. **Technology is a veil, never a wall.** The data-sky is transparent — glyphs laid thinly over real clouds, real light. The world remains fully visible through the layer. Tech may *annotate* reality; it may never obstruct, darken, or replace it. (Contrast: cyberpunk, where the interface eats the sky.)

3. **The hand stays visible.** Halftone dots. Painterly canopy. Airbrush grain. Every image passed through a *craft process* — printing, painting, spraying — and wears the evidence proudly. Nothing is clinically rendered. This is the visual form of the humble craftsman: process as honesty.

4. **Old beauty is allowed.** Roman arches, classical statues, Michelangelo's hands. The world being noticed includes the ancient — run through a modern process (riso-style duotone, Klein blue). This is the two registers as image-making: the philosophy *and* the print shop. Kebab's visual world can quote the canon without irony, as long as the process humbles it.

5. **Light is the emotional subject.** Sun through grass, haze on the sea, chartreuse breaking through canopy. Everything is luminous, soft, daylit. The mood is not excitement — it is *calm awe*. The feeling of a good long walk during which you had three thoughts worth keeping.

---

## 2. The structural move: the room and the window

The references are daylight; the app is a near-black quiet room. This is not a conflict — it is the brand's architecture:

- **The world (marketing, brand surfaces): daylight.** Meadows, canopies, villas, the data-sky. Where thoughts are *caught*. This is where the flinch happens.
- **The room (the app): night.** `#171718`, the dark pocket, the yellow note glowing. Where thoughts are *kept*. Good at 2am.

The brand's signature composition is the two together: **the dark object in the daylit world** — the kebab UI floating over the meadow, the phone-dark screenshot framed by sky, the yellow sticky note lying in the grass. Day outside, night inside. You live out there; you keep things in here. Every App Store screenshot, website section, and poster can be built on this one relationship.

---

## 3. Palette

**The room (unchanged — protect it):**
- Background `#171718` · text `#CAD0DB` · sticky-note yellow `#EBCF47` · send purple `#935ED5` · resurface amber `#FFC57C` · fire pink `#F79CE1`. Color = intention; jewels appear only when you act.

**The world (new — marketing layer only):**
- **Meadow:** deep teal `≈#1E5C52`, canopy green `≈#3E7A3C`, chartreuse light `≈#C6D64B` — note how close chartreuse sits to sticky-yellow; that adjacency is the bridge between world and room.
- **Print:** Klein/riso blue `≈#2244CC`–`#0038E0` on warm cream `≈#F4EDDA`. Always as *duotone* — one ink on one paper, never as a full-color scheme.
- **Sky:** hazed blue `≈#7A9BC4` to white light.

Rule: the world palette never enters the app. The room palette (especially the yellow) may appear in the world. The yellow note is the recurring visitor — the one object that exists on both sides.

---

## 4. Motif library

Recurring, ownable images. Use these instead of inventing new ones per asset:

- **The data-sky.** Monospace glyphs (JetBrains Mono — the wordmark's own face) laid at low opacity over sky, light, or landscape. This is *thought over the world*: the annotation layer of an attentive mind. Density stays low; the world always wins. This is the brand's tech signifier — never circuits, never neon, never glass HUDs.
- **The skewer in the wild.** Small unlike things threaded on one line: five white birds in formation; a vertical feed-line threading a quote, a photo, an ice-cream cone, a link card. The composition device for any "what is kebab" surface.
- **The frame.** Arches, windows, loggias, door-frames — architecture whose job is to hold a view. Where a screenshot needs a container, it gets a frame that implies *looking*, not a device mockup on a desk.
- **The observer.** A single figure, back turned, facing a horizon. Never a face, never a phone in hand, never a smile at a screen. The user is the one *seeing*, not the one being seen.
- **The yellow note in the dark / in the grass.** The humble analog artifact as the light source of a dark room, or the human trace in a vast landscape.
- **The hands.** Reaching, offering, catching — airbrushed, classical-quoting. Reserved for the *sharing* layer when it ships: a thought passed from one hand toward another. (The Klein-blue reference is the seed.)

## 5. Process rules (how things are made to look)

- Everything passes through a visible process: halftone screens, grain, paint, print texture. If an asset looks like a default Figma export, it isn't finished.
- Duotone is the print signature: one ink, warm paper. Rotate ink colors rarely (Klein blue is home; sticky-yellow-on-charcoal is the in-house variant).
- Photography, when used, obeys the reference light: natural, hazed, luminous — the world mid-notice. No studio, no stock, no desks.
- Renders and illustration may quote the painterly (canopy) or the printed (villa), never the glossy-3D or the vector-corporate (no Memphis blobs, no flat "startup illustration" people).
- Type in the world: DM Sans speaks, JetBrains Mono labels and annotates (captions, coordinates, timestamps, the data-sky itself). Mono is the voice of the annotation layer everywhere.

---

## 6. What this world is *not* (the negative space)

- Not cyberpunk: no neon on black streets, no rain, no glitch, no HUD chrome.
- Not dystopia's opposite-twin, corporate optimism: no gradient-mesh unicorn pastels, no confetti.
- Not nostalgia: the halftone villas are *processed* old beauty, not sepia longing. The future is the subject; the past is material.
- Not wellness-spa minimalism: kebab is calm but it has wit, ink, and street food in it. A little grain, a little humor, always.
- Not productivity-tool imagery: no dashboards in space, no brains made of nodes, no lightbulbs.

## 7. Applications

**Website:** dark-room base (the site *is* the app's world at rest), daylight imagery breaking in through "windows" — full-bleed meadow/data-sky sections as you scroll, the UI floating dark against them. Hero: the dark composer over the luminous world; one yellow note somewhere.

**App Store screenshots:** the room-and-window composition — dark UI screens framed against the daylight world (meadow, canopy, sky) rather than on flat color cards. Captions in DM Sans, annotations in mono. The set should read as one continuous landscape when swiped through — a skewer of screens.

**The launch film (the crescendo scene, now art-directed):** a walk through the noticed world — park light, city edges, the data-sky faintly present as thought builds (mono glyphs condensing in the air as the idea compounds, strings rising) — record-scratch — *"wait, what did my nephew want for his birthday?"* — cut to the dark room: both thoughts land in kebab, one above the other. The ice cream and the philosophy, one skewer. End card: yellow note on black. *kebab — a place for thoughts you don't want to lose.*

**Social / changelog headers:** the duotone print system — each update gets a one-ink halftone image (a villa, a bird, a hand, an object from the update) with mono annotations. Cheap to produce, instantly recognizable, collectible over time — the changelog becomes a print series.

**Founding-member materials:** the print system at its most crafted — a numbered duotone card ("founding member of kebab · No. 0041"), digital or literal. The wine-that-appreciates story in object form.

## 8. The UI refresh — direction and one caution

**The caution first, as doctrine: the app is the room. Do not flood it with daylight.** The dark quiet interior is *why* kebab feels like a refuge at 2am; repainting the product to match the marketing meadow would trade the product's soul for its poster. The daylight world is where kebab is *seen*; the dark room is where kebab is *lived in*. Refresh the room's craft, not its weather.

What the references *should* change in-app — texture, warmth, and the annotation layer:

1. **The data-sky comes inside, at whisper volume.** Mono-glyph texture at very low opacity as the atmosphere of *empty* moments: empty states, the welcome screen sky behind the sticky note, loading, the space above the first entry. Thought-weather in the room.
2. **Craft grain.** A near-invisible paper/print grain on backgrounds and sheets, so surfaces feel made rather than rendered. The Liquid Glass composer already floats; give the room walls a material.
3. **Empty states and onboarding get world-art.** These are the app's only marketing surfaces — the duotone/painterly world belongs there: a small framed image (an arch, birds, grass) above the copy. First-run can literally be a window: daylight image → you enter the room.
4. **Mono as the annotation voice in-app.** Timestamps, breadcrumbs, counters — the small true labels — migrate toward mono, unifying the in-app annotation layer with the data-sky motif. (Body text stays DM Sans; the room speaks human.)
5. **The yellow note earns more moments.** It appears at welcome and then vanishes from the product. Let the analog artifact recur where warmth is needed: founding-member badge, milestone moments ("one year of kebab"), the paywall's human touch.
6. **Celebration in jewels, not confetti.** Any delight animation draws from the existing jewel colors and the world's motifs (a small flock, a thread pulling taut) — never generic sparkle.

## 9. Standing tests for any new visual

- **The attention test:** is this an image *of noticing*, or an image of a product? (If a device or a face-lit-by-screen is the subject, kill it.)
- **The veil test:** does the tech layer let the world through? (If the interface eats the sky, it's the wrong future.)
- **The hand test:** can you see a process — print, paint, grain? (If it looks like a default export, it isn't done.)
- **The room-and-window test:** is it clear which side of the glass we're on — daylit world or dark room — and does the composition honor the relationship?
- **The two-registers test (inherited):** ice cream next to philosophy. If the image is all marble and no mint chip, it's become pretentious.
